My term as the President of the Software Development Club has come to an end. Over the past year, in addition to leading the club, I also served as the team leader for numerous group projects—such as Physics Inquiry, Chemistry Inquiry, and school-required courses. Throughout these experiences, I gained significant insights into leadership.

## What Is Leadership?

Leadership is the ability to coordinate a team and guide them toward a shared goal. It typically demands strong leadership traits along with effective management strategies.

## How I Manage a Team

Whether managing club officers or heading a group project, every team requires an explicit division of labor. In a club setting, roles must be clearly defined—who manages finances, who organizes events, and so on. Unclear authority and responsibilities must be strictly avoided. For instance, in my role as President and Instructional Lead, I refrained from interfering with the treasurer's financial management. Doing so could weaken the treasurer's authority and diminish their motivation.

The same logic applies to group projects. Tasks must be explicitly designated—such as who works on which presentation slide, or who takes on specific duties during lab experiments (e.g., recording data, operating equipment, or cleaning up). Consequently, my team was consistently the first in class to finish experiments and reports, maintaining an error-free record with zero data discrepancies.

## Personal Reflections

Serving as a leader taught me a great deal, especially regarding the management principles mentioned above. Nevertheless, I recognize that I still have much to learn. For example, when a teammate failed to finish their task, I often stepped in to complete it myself. In practice, this should not happen: once responsibilities are assigned, the assigned individual should see them through. I realized that picking up the slack might inadvertently encourage members to disengage, relying on the leader to finish everything—a counterproductive dynamic.

Additionally, I encountered situations where certain members were unwilling to perform their assigned work. Although I should have held them accountable to finish promptly, I often reassigned their work to other team members and gave the uncooperative individual simpler tasks (like taking photos) to protect our overall efficiency and grades. In hindsight, this was not an ideal solution and remains a key area for my personal improvement.

作為軟體研究社的社長任期已經結束了，在過往一年內，除了社團的社長，還擔任了許多小組報告的組長，例如物理探究、化學探究或者是校定必修等等，在此期間，我也認知到了許多關於領導力的事情。

## 何謂領導力
領導力即是能夠統籌一個團隊、領導一個團隊前往目標的能力，通常是需要有領袖特質以及諸多管理方式的能力。

## 我是如何管理團隊？
在小組報告或者是社團幹部，但凡是一個團隊，就必須有極其明確的分工，在社團中，必須明確誰管錢、誰辦理活動，絕對不能有權責不清的事情發生，像我就是擔任社長與教學，我就絕對不會去干涉總務長所管理的錢財，如果發生了，這可能會弱化總務長的權力，並導致擔任意願低下。

在小組報告中同理，必須明確分工誰做哪一頁簡報，或者是在實驗時誰是負責什麼工作，紀錄、操作、洗器材等等，因此在做實驗或報告的時候，我這一組永遠是全班最快做完的，且沒有數據錯誤或各種錯誤的情況發生。

## 個人心得
在擔任這些領袖的時候我也是學到了很多，例如說前述所提到的管理團隊的方法，雖然作為一個領袖，我需要學習的仍然很多，例如說如果有人做不完我還是會靠自己把它做完，通常是不應該發生這種事情的，既然已經明確分工了，誰負責就應該是誰把它做完，我也思考到這樣可能會導致組員不做，並最後交給組長做就好，是一個不好的心態。

在擔任組長的過程中，也會有組員不願意進行他的工作，雖然我應該要要求他盡速完成，但為了避免影響效率跟影響其他組員的分數，我還是會將他的工作分配給組員完成，並交辦給他其他簡單的工作，例如照相等，這樣似乎並不是一個好的方法，是一個需要檢討的部分。
